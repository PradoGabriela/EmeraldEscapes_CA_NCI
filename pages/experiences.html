Fionn decided to build a giant stone road across the sea so he could meet his enemy. But when he saw how BIG Benandonner was, he got scared and ran home!

Luckily, Fionn’s clever wife, Oonagh, had a plan. She dressed Fionn up as a giant baby and tucked him into a cradle. When Benandonner arrived, he saw the huge baby and thought, “If the baby is this big, how enormous must the father be?”

Terrified, Benandonner ran all the way back to Scotland, breaking the stone road behind him. The stones left behind became the Giant’s Causeway, a magical place where the legend of the two giants lives on!

One day, Aoife used her magic to turn the four children into beautiful white swans! She placed a spell on them that would last for 900 years. They would spend 300 years on a lake, 300 years on the cold sea, and 300 years near the coast of County Mayo.

And so, the story of the Children of Lir became one of Ireland’s most famous legends, reminding us of the love between family and the strength of staying together through difficult times.

Long ago, beside the River Shannon in County Clare, there was a place where Vikings came to trade. Around the year 970, a Viking trading camp stood where Bunratty Castle is today!

Over the years, several castles were built on this very spot. The castle we see today was built by the MacNamara family around 1425. Later, it became the home of the powerful O’Brien family, who ruled the area as the Earls of Thomond.

Inside the castle, people ate grand meals, celebrated special occasions, and lived behind strong stone walls. But life was not always peaceful! The castle saw battles and changes of power as different groups fought to control the land.

After many years, the castle was left empty and began to fall into ruin. Luckily, in the 1950s, a man named Lord Gort helped save it. He worked with others to repair the castle and bring it back to life.

Today, Bunratty Castle stands proudly for visitors to explore. You can discover its old rooms, imagine life in medieval times, and visit the nearby Folk Park to see how people lived long ago.

So, when you walk through Bunratty Castle, imagine the knights, lords, and families who once called it home. What stories do you think these ancient stone walls could tell?

Gráinne was not afraid to stand up for herself. One famous story says that when she was a girl, she wanted to sail with her father. He told her that her long hair might get caught in the ship’s ropes, so she cut it short! This is how she got the nickname Gráinne Mhaol.

Galway City and County Galway

Celtic Legends Weekend

Experience Irish culture through legendary stories, traditional music, colourful streets, and a memorable weekend in Galway.

3 days / 2 nights From €199

The Story Behind the Experience

Irish folklore has been passed down through generations through stories, music and shared traditions. Explore Galway's lively streets, learn about the origins of Irish legends and experience the cultural atmosphere that keeps these traditions alive.

What's Included?

  • Exploration of Galway city
  • Introduction to Irish folklore and traditions
  • Storytelling session
  • Evening of traditional music in a local setting
  • Group cultural activities

Duration: 3 days / 2 nights

Location: Galway city and surrounding County Galway

Suitable for: Friends, solo travellers and small groups

Requirements: Advance booking; minimum group size may apply

Availability: Year-round, selected weekends

Price: €199–€349 per person

Long ago, before televisions, phones, and the internet, people in Ireland loved gathering around a warm fire to share stories. Families, friends, and neighbours listened to tales about brave heroes, magical fairies, mysterious creatures, and faraway lands.

Some storytellers were known as seanchaithe (pronounced “shan-a-kee”). They were keepers of stories who remembered old legends, family histories, and local traditions. They travelled from place to place, sharing their stories with everyone they met.

These stories were passed from grandparents to parents, and from parents to children. Every storyteller could add their own special touch, so a story might change a little each time it was told!

Even today, Irish storytellers keep this wonderful tradition alive. They share old tales in schools, festivals, pubs, and gatherings, helping new generations discover the magic of Ireland's past.

As you explore Ireland, look at its ancient castles, green hills, quiet lakes, and winding roads. Every place has a history, and every family has stories worth sharing.

So, here is a little challenge for you: when your Irish adventure comes to an end, why not share your favourite legend with someone else? You could become a storyteller too, keeping the magic of Ireland alive for years to come!
